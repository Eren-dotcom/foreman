The problem

Agents already pay per unit of work.
There is no way to do it safely.

Hot wallet
A funded key an agent spends from. One buggy or malicious provider and the budget is gone.

Custodian
A platform holds the money. KYC, account, chargeback windows — and it can't settle a tenth of a cent.

x402 (HTTP 402 for agents, a Linux Foundation project with Visa, Mastercard, Stripe, Google, AWS) has three documented holes: no refunds or disputes, no per-provider spend ceiling, and no BCH settlement. Coinbase's own head of AI product says escrow and refunds are the missing piece.

The primitive

One covenant per job.

agent ──funds 100,000 sat──► [ pod ]
                               │
  provider delivers ───────────┤ deliver()  → pays a whole chunk, rest returns to the pod
  provider over-reaches ───────┤ deliver()  → ✗ refused by the VM (window)
  payment redirected ──────────┤ deliver()  → ✗ refused (payee pinned)
  client panics mid-job ───────┤ refund()   → ✗ refused until expiry
  job stops, window closes ────┘ refund()   → everything left goes back to the agent

contract(pod) = ChunkPod(clientPkh, providerPkh, chunk, window, validUntil)

What the VM enforces

Not policy. Code.

the provider cannot:

  • exceed the window in one tx
  • take a partial chunk
  • redirect its own payment
  • skim via a third output
  • short-change the remainder
  • merge two pods

the client cannot:

  • reclaim before expiry
  • refund to anyone else
  • skip the provider's final chunk in a close
  • leave the pod short by a satoshi

16 covenant tests   every refusal is pinned by a test that runs the real BCH VM, with the exact failing line named.

Before the money moves

A refusal that costs nothing.

intent ──► guard (9 rules, live chain state) ──► deny: deliver.window
                                                    │
                                                    └─ 0 sat spent · 0 tx built · no relay contacted

the same intent id again ──► the same preceipt, byte for byte  (duplicate: true)

The covenant is the authority. The guard is the courtesy that keeps a bad job from ever reaching it — and every preceipt is a hashed record of a decision made before a transaction existed, so an agent's reasoning is auditable even when it decided not to act.

After the money moves

The books answer to the chain.

funded = paid + fees + refunded + elsewhere + held        ← must close to the satoshi

3/3 pods reconcile       1/3 sets of books agreed
                         └─ our own close() paid a final chunk the ledger never booked

Reconciliation ignores Foreman's ledger and rebuilds settlement by walking real transactions. It found a bug in this app within its first hour — on a chain that was correct the whole time. The covenant never paid the wrong amount; the paperwork did.
